VAM

Mycorrhiza are beneficial fungi that form a symbiotic (mutual) relationships with plant roots.

Vesicular-Arbuscular Mycorrhiza (VAM) is the most common type and enters into the root cells, forming structures known as:

Vesicles – for nutrient storage

Arbuscules – for nutrient exchange

These fungi increase the plant's ability to absorb water and essential nutrients (especially phosphorus, zinc, and copper) from the soil. They are beneficial for most crops except for non-mycorrhizal families like Brassicaceae (cabbage family) .

Feature Benefit

Symbiotic with Roots Enhances water and nutrient uptake (especially P, Zn, Cu, Fe, Mg)

Increased Root Surface Area Extends root zone up to 100x via fungal hyphae

Drought Resistance Plants tolerate water stress better

Reduced Fertilizer Dependency Improves soil nutrient availability and efficiency

Better Soil Structure Fungal hyphae improve soil aggregation and aeration

Compatible with Most Crops Works well with vegetables, cereals, fruit trees, ornamentals, etc.

Sustainable & Organic 100% natural; promotes ecological farming

Item Quantity & Notes

VAM Inoculum (starter culture) 1–2 kg (commercial powder/granular form)

Sterilized Sand + Soil Mix 50% sand + 50% sieved soil (sterilized if possible) or vermiculite

Host Plants (e.g., Maize, Onion, Bajra, Ragi) 30–40 seedlings for 10 trays/bed

Plastic trays or raised nursery beds 1 m² per batch

Water (chlorine-free) Regular irrigation required

Shade Net / Controlled Environment For uniform growth and fungus colonization

Step-by-step (takes about 60 days):

Prepare Medium:

Mix sterilized soil and sand in 1:1 ratio and fill into trays or raised nursery beds.

Plant Host Plants:

Sow or transplant seedlings of host plants (e.g., maize, onion, bajra) into the medium.

Inoculate with VAM:

Sprinkle or mix 100–200 g of VAM inoculum around root zones.

Watering & Maintenance:

Water gently every day.

Keep under shade or partial sun.

Allow plants to grow for 45–60 days .

Harvest Inoculum:

After 60 days, uproot plants and cut roots into small pieces .

Mix roots + soil + sand thoroughly.

This mixture is your on-farm multiplied VAM inoculum .

🔹 Seed Treatment:

Mix 100 g VAM with 1 kg seeds.

Use sticker (e.g., rice gruel, jaggery solution) to help adhere.

🔹 Root Dipping (for Transplants):

Mix 500 g VAM with 10–15 liters water + cow dung slurry.

Dip roots for 15–30 mins before transplanting.

🔹 Soil Application:

Trees: 100–200 g per tree near the root zone.

Vegetables/Row Crops: 5–10 kg per acre mixed with FYM/compost, applied in root zone.

🔹 Pot Plants:

25–50 g per pot during repotting or around root area.

These crops do not form mycorrhizal associations (they’re called non-mycorrhizal plants):

Crop Type Examples Notes

Brassicaceae (Mustard family) Mustard, Cabbage, Cauliflower, Broccoli, Radish Contain compounds that inhibit fungi

Chenopodiaceae Spinach, Beetroot Non-mycorrhizal root system

Amaranthaceous Amaranthus, Red Spinach Roots do not allow mycorrhizal colonization

Lupin species Lupins (a type of legume) Produce compounds that inhibit colonization

Orchidaceous Orchids Require specific fungal partners, not VAM

Brassicaceae & Chenopodiaceae families naturally produce compounds that inhibit mycorrhizal colonization .

These crops rely on different microbial interactions and do not benefit from VAM.

Apply at nursery stage or during transplanting .

Combine with organic manure or compost for best results.

Avoid recent fungicide use where VAM is applied.

Fruit trees, especially woody perennials , naturally form long-term relationships with mycorrhizal fungi . This helps them survive in poor, dry, or phosphorus-deficient soils .

Apply during planting : Mix 50–200g VAM in soil at the root zone.

With compost/FYM : VAM works better with organic material.

Avoid fungicides during and after application for 10–15 days.

Flowers like marigold, rose, chrysanthemum, and gladiolus have fibrous root systems that readily support mycorrhizal colonization .

This leads to improved nutrient and water uptake , higher quality blooms , and greater stress resistance .

Nursery stage : Mix 20–50g VAM per seedling tray or pot.

Field planting : Apply 100–200g VAM per square meter of bed or per planting pit.

Always apply with organic compost or vermicompost , and avoid fungicides for 10–15 days after application.

High-phosphorus soils : Even in mycorrhizal crops, if the soil already has very high phosphorus (P), VAM may be less effective .

Chemical fungicides : Recent use of soil fungicides may reduce the VAM viability in any crop.

Crop Stage Application Type

Before Sowing Seed Treatment

Transplanting Time Root Dipping

Early Vegetative Soil Application (basal)

Annual Maintenance Top dressing near roots

Do not use chemical fungicides (e.g., carbendazim) near the time of VAM application.

Works best with organic soil and compost .

Store VAM inoculum in cool, dry conditions away from direct sunlight.

One-time application gives long-term benefits due to self-multiplication in roots.
